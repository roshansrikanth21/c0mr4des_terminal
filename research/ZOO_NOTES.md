# Strategy zoo: how to read the results

`ZOO_RESULTS.md` is generated by `zoo_report.py`. This file holds what the tables cannot say.

## What was tested

- **A01-A28, buying options intraday.** A textbook index signal picks the direction; the trade buys one lot (65) of the at-the-money NIFTY weekly option at the signal bar's close and exits on the index stop, the index target, a signal exit, or 15:15. One trade a day per strategy, every trading day including expiry days.
- **B01-B11, selling options intraday.** Straddles, strangles, an iron fly, an iron condor and a credit spread, with per-leg or combined stops checked on 5-minute closes.
- **C01-C11, positional.** Daily closes of the NIFTY ETF, the gold ETF and today's NIFTY 50 stocks; the pairs book follows the pasted rule (enter at z = +-2, exit at 0, stop at 3.5 or 30 days).

Option prices are real 5-minute prints for 96-100% of exits in A and the naked B books. Costs are the project's own charges plus 0.5% slippage a side on options, 0.18% a side on cash, 0.05% a side per leg on pairs.

## Rulebook, fixed before any result

Survive = net positive over 2021-2026, at least 4 of 6 calendar years profitable, maximum drawdown under half the capital basis. Then pick on 2021-2024 and judge on 2025-2026.

## Caveats that change the reading

1. **Returns are simple, not compounded,** on a fixed capital basis: Rs 50,000 for a bought lot, Rs 2,00,000 for naked short options, Rs 60,000 for hedged structures, Rs 1,00,000 for the daily books. The margin figures are round assumptions, not broker quotes.
2. **C08 and C09 are inflated by survivorship.** They pick from today's NIFTY 50 members, which already contains the stocks that did well enough to be in the index. C08 fell to 2.1% a year on 2025-2026.
3. **B04 and B05 are low-confidence.** Only 46-68% of their exits were real prints, because far wings fall outside the downloaded +-7 strikes. They fail by a wide margin either way.
4. **The pairs book needs stock futures.** One pair is two futures positions of several lakh notional each. The 2.8% a year is on per-side notional; it is not reachable with a small account.
5. **Stops are checked on 5-minute closes.** A spike inside a bar is seen only at its close. That is conservative when price keeps running and blind to a spike that reverses.
6. **Fifty tries.** About one strategy would look significant by luck alone. Three have a monthly t-statistic above 2: C08 (biased, see 2), C10 and B07.

## Stress test of B07, the expiry-day short straddle (diagnostic, nothing was re-selected)

| Change | Profit factor |
|---|---|
| Stop per leg 15% to 75% | 1.46 to 1.73 |
| No stop | 1.07 |
| Entry 09:20 (as registered) | 1.50 |
| Entry 09:30 / 09:45 / 10:00 / 11:00 / 12:00 | 1.27 / 1.17 / 1.30 / 1.48 / 1.40 |
| Slippage 0% / 1% / 2% a side | 1.58 / 1.42 / 1.28 |
| Thursday expiries, to Aug 2025 (242 trades) | 1.61 |
| Tuesday expiries, from Sep 2025 (63 trades) | 1.17 |
| Hedged as an iron fly, wings 100 to 300 points | 0.98 to 1.14 |

Reading: the edge is not an artefact of one stop level or of costs, but 09:20 is the best of the entry times tried, so 1.2-1.5 is a fairer expectation than 1.5. It is weaker since expiry moved to Tuesday. The hedged version a small account could afford does not carry the edge. One lot collects about Rs 6,800 of credit, averages about Rs 530 a trade, and lost Rs 9,374 on its worst day.

## Bottom line

- Buying options intraday on textbook signals: 0 of 28 survive, 27 of 28 lose money.
- What survives is positional ETF allocation, which a small account can run but which pays in line with or below simply holding the index (except the NIFTY-or-gold rotation, carried by gold in 2025), and expiry-day option selling, which needs about Rs 2 lakh of margin and carries gap risk.
