# Prop-firm route: how to read the results

`PROP_RESULTS.md` is generated by `prop_challenge.py`. This file holds what the tables cannot say.

## What a prop firm is, for this project

You pay a fee (roughly $90-155 for a $10k account, about $540 for $100k) to trade a simulated account under rules. The common two-step form: make +10%, then +5%, without losing 5% in a day or 10% in total. Pass, and the firm pays you about 80% of later profits. The most you can lose is the fee. Industry figures put passes at roughly 5-15% of buyers and payouts at about 7%.

## What was tested

- Seven markets: EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, AUDUSD, gold, S&P 500, Nasdaq 100. Free one-minute history from HistData, 2020 to Nov 2026, turned into hourly bars.
- Fifteen textbook strategies (`prop_zoo.py`): session breakouts, the "liquidity sweep" fades from the ICT material, hourly trend and mean-reversion, three daily ones. Rules fixed before any result.
- Each trade risks 1R. Costs are a round-trip spread plus commission per market.
- Pick on 2021-2024, judge once on 2025-2026.

## Result

- **14 of 15 lose after costs**, in both periods. Every intraday strategy is among them, including both liquidity-sweep fades (PF 0.79-0.82 on 2025-26).
- **One survives: S12, the daily RSI-2 pullback.** Above the 200-day average, buy after a sharp two-day dip; exit when price closes above its 5-day average, after 5 days, or at a 2-ATR stop. Mirror for shorts.
  - 2021-24: 315 trades, +0.105R each, PF 1.57, all four years positive, t = 3.2.
  - 2025-26: 151 trades, +0.070R each, PF 1.34, t = 1.5. Positive, but not statistically settled.
  - Most of the unseen-period profit is the two US indices (PF 4.25 and 2.05). EURUSD and GBPUSD lost.
- Strategy-by-market pairs that looked good on the build years held up 50% of the time afterwards, which is what luck gives. Picking the best market per strategy is not a method.

## The challenge simulation

At 1% risk a trade, S12 passes both phases in about 71% of simulated attempts drawn from 2025-26 results, but the median pass takes about two years. At 2% risk: 73% pass, median about 11 months, 41% inside a year. Average payout over the first 180 funded days, per challenge bought, is 2-4% of the account, against a 0.6% fee.

## Caveats that change the reading

1. **A coin flip also comes out ahead on paper.** With no edge and no costs, 31-35% of attempts pass and the average payout beats the fee, because the trader keeps 80% of the upside and loses only the fee. With realistic costs that mostly disappears. So a positive number in the "Net" column says little about skill; the firm's rules exist to close exactly this gap.
2. **Rules I did not model.** Loss limits are checked on closed trades, not on open equity. No consistency rule, no news or weekend-holding restrictions, no minimum-day rule, no payout refusal. Each of these makes the real thing harder than the table.
3. **Overnight financing is ignored.** S12 holds for days. On index CFDs financing costs roughly 0.02-0.03R per trade, a quarter to a third of its edge. Some firms also forbid holding over the weekend on the cheaper account types.
4. **S12 is a known pattern.** It is published (Connors) and works mainly because US indices have risen. It is long-biased in practice; a flat or falling index decade would look different.
5. **Hourly bars.** A bar that touches both stop and target counts as a stop. Entries are at bar closes.
6. **Fifteen tries, one pass.** Luck alone would give about one false pass at this bar.

## India

Paying a challenge fee to trade a simulated account is described as a grey area: the Liberalised Remittance Scheme bars sending money abroad for margin trading, and there is no clear RBI ruling on evaluation fees. Payouts are foreign income and must be declared. This needs a chartered accountant's answer before any fee is paid; nothing here is legal advice.

## Bottom line

The prop route does change the capital problem: the loss is capped at the fee and the account size is no longer the constraint. It does not change the edge problem. Intraday rules fail here exactly as they did on NIFTY. The one candidate is a slow daily strategy that would take about a year to pass a challenge and whose unseen-period evidence is still thin. The free next step is to run it forward on paper under the challenge rules before buying anything.
