# Dip-buying on ETFs: the reading

`DIP_ETF_RESULTS.md` is generated by `dip_etf.py`. Pass marks were fixed before the first run.

## What passed and what did not

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| The rule has an edge | Real: 7,023 trades over 26 years, +0.17% a trade after costs, t-statistic 6.6. Positive in 20 of 27 years (74%; the mark was 75%), so formally not passed. |
| As an unlevered five-slot portfolio | Not passed: +2.9% a year, worst drawdown 26%. Holding SPY made 8.8% a year. |
| The learning layer | **Passed on the first try**: each year's model, trained only on earlier years, picked dips that beat the ones it skipped in 13 of 17 years. Its picks: 2,681 trades, PF 1.33, t 5.7. Skipped: PF 1.05. |

This is the only place in the project where a learning model clearly worked out of sample, and it worked because the rule underneath has an edge to sort. On intraday rules there was nothing to sort.

## Why it still is not a way to make money

The edge is small and the trades bunch up. ETFs dip together, so on a bad day many positions stop out at once.

Model's picks, 2010-2026, in risk units (not in the generated file; computed from `data/dip_trades.pkl`):

| Sizing | Trades a year | Avg R | t-stat | R a year | Worst year | At 1% risk: return a year / worst drawdown / worst day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Take every pick | 159 | +0.079 | 5.9 | 12.5 | -36.5R | +12.5% / -43.8% / -18.9% |
| At most 5 open | 104 | +0.034 | 2.0 | 3.5 | -19.7R | +3.5% / -33.2% / -5.5% |
| At most 3 open | 77 | +0.024 | 1.2 | 1.8 | -13.3R | +1.8% / -22.3% / -4.5% |

- Taking every pick earns the most but would breach any prop firm's 5% daily and 10% total loss limits, and no cash account can hold 15+ positions at full size.
- Capping the number of open positions makes it survivable and removes most of the return.
- In the prop-challenge simulation (two-step, 5% daily / 10% total loss): 30-40% of attempts pass both phases; average payout in the first 180 funded days is 1.6-2.2% of the account per challenge bought, against a 0.6% fee. A no-edge coin flip with costs scored 0.4-1.1% in the same simulation. Positive, slim, and it leaves out the firm's other rules.

## Bottom line

Dip-buying in an uptrend is a real, durable, small edge, and a model can sharpen it. Without leverage it earns less than holding the index. With leverage its clustered losses are what kill accounts. It is not the income source the project was looking for.
