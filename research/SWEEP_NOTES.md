## Findings

**A. Crypto.** 13 of 16 lose. The mean-reversion and session rules lose worse than on forex. The three that passed the build years are all trend-following; two stayed positive on 2025-26, both weakly (profit factor 1.14, t-statistics 0.9 and 0.4). Funding payments are not charged, and trend-following holds for days, so the real figure is lower. This is a "maybe", not a result.

**B. Fair-value gaps.** Lose on every market group: profit factor 0.84-0.90 over 26,000 trades. Together with the two liquidity-sweep rules in the prop study, every ICT-style rule tested has lost.

**C. Opening-range breakout in New York.** Both versions lose. The reel's small-target version wins 81% of the time and still has a profit factor of 0.34-0.42: many small wins, a few full-range losses. Same shape as on NIFTY.

**D. The daily RSI-2 pullback over 36 years.** This is the most useful result in the sweep.
- All seven markets, 1990-2026: 2,199 trades, profit factor 1.28, t-statistic 4.7. Positive in every period.
- The two US indices alone: profit factor 1.15 / 1.76 / 1.39 / 2.08 / 1.71 across the five periods. Never below 1.
- Forex and gold alone: 0.88 / 1.49 / 1.04 / 1.17 / 1.02. Close to nothing since 2010.
- Longs 1.36, shorts 1.14.
- So the "it is really an index strategy" pattern seen on 2025-26 was already there in 1990-2020, on data that played no part in choosing it. Focusing it on the indices is now supported by independent evidence, not just by hindsight.
- Limits: cash-index daily bars (a prop account trades the CFD), and financing is still not charged.

**E. US pairs.** +2.5% a year on per-side capital, five of six years positive, worst drawdown 8%. Real but small, and it needs shorting. Same as the Indian version (+2.8%).

**F. Index-or-gold rotation, full history.** This corrects something said earlier.
- India 2010-2026: rotation 13.7% a year against 11.2% for holding the NIFTY ETF, with a 26% worst drawdown against 43%.
- US 2005-2026: rotation 10.4% against 11.7% for holding SPY, with a 37% worst drawdown against 70%.
- The 17-18% a year quoted from the six-year Indian study was the 2021-2026 window, which contains the 2025 gold run. Over the long run the rule earns about what the index earns. What it reliably does is roughly halve the worst drawdown.
- In most years it simply holds the index, so the "beat the index" count is low by construction.
- Yahoo's Indian ETF history has bad ticks; daily moves above 15% were removed.

**G. Copying investors.** Berkshire since 2005: +917% against +837% for the S&P 500 ETF; over the last ten years it is slightly behind. The Congress-trades ETF: +104% against +95% since February 2023. Neither is meaningfully different from buying the index.

## What changes

1. Intraday is closed on every market tested: NIFTY, SENSEX, forex, gold, US indices, crypto. About 65 rules, none survive.
2. The one trading rule with long evidence is the daily pullback on US indices.
3. The ETF rotation is a drawdown reducer, not a return booster. Buying and holding the index ETF is as good on return.
4. Crypto trend-following is the only new candidate, and its evidence is weak.
